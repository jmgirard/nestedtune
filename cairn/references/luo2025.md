## Terminology note

**Read `luo2026.md` first.** That page establishes the black-box testing
framework and the EvaluateAlg/EvaluateModel split; this one applies the same
machinery to a different target — not "how good is the algorithm" but "is the
algorithm *stable*".

The stability notion tested here (Definition 1, p. 3) is
**(ε, δ)-stability**: dropping one training point changes the prediction at a
fresh test point by more than ε with probability at most δ. Note carefully:
this is a perturbation of the **prediction**, not of the **loss**. The paper
says so explicitly in its Discussion (p. 9) and names the loss-based
alternative — Bousquet & Elisseeff's *hypothesis stability* — as outside its
scope, with a unified treatment listed as an open question. **This distinction
is load-bearing for anything the package might conclude; see Bearing below.**

Two refinements of the black-box notion appear:

- **Transparent models** — the test may call 𝒜 but may inspect the fitted
  models analytically. Definition 2.
- **Black-box models** — the fitted models may only be probed at finitely many
  test points, subject to a second budget B_eval. Definition 3, Theorem 3.

## What it establishes

**Result 1 (Theorem 1, p. 6) — three simultaneous constraints, and power is
bounded by the smallest.** For any black-box test with training budget B_train
that is assumption-free valid at level α, and any (𝒜, P, n) that *is*
(ε, δ)-stable with δ*_ε the smallest δ that holds, power is bounded by the
minimum of

| Constraint | Bound | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| Computational | α·((1−δ*_ε)/(1−δ))^{B_train/n} | Not enough calls to 𝒜 |
| Statistical, Y | α·((1−δ*_ε)/(1−δ))^{N_ℓ/n} ÷ (1 − B_train/\|Y\|)∧1 | Not enough labelled data, and no budget to exhaust Y |
| Statistical, X | α·((1−δ*_ε)/(1−δ̃))^{(N_ℓ+N_u)/n} ÷ (1 − B_train/\|X\|)∧1 | Same for X, helped by unlabelled data |

with δ̃ = (δ + 1/n) ∧ 1. High power requires **all three** to be large:
B_train ≫ n, and either N_ℓ ≫ n or |Y| small enough to search exhaustively, and
either N_ℓ + N_u ≫ n or |X| small enough likewise.

**Result 2 — the unified message.** With limited data, exhaustive search over
the data space is essentially the only universally valid way to certify
stability (Abstract; Discussion, p. 9) — and exhaustive search is precisely
what a computational budget forbids. This is the sentence the paper is built
around.

**Result 3 (Theorem 2, p. 7) — restricting to deterministic algorithms buys
almost nothing.** Under the weaker validity requirement that covers only
deterministic algorithms, and provided no single data point carries mass ≥ 0.2,
the bound becomes (α + C/n)·((1−δ*_ε)/(1−δ−1/n))^{B_train/n} for a universal
constant C. Same shape, an O(1/n) slack.

**Result 4 (Proposition 1, p. 8) — again, a trivial split test is nearly
optimal.** Build ⌊κ⌋ independent copies of (n training points + one test
point), where κ = min(B_train/n, N_ℓ/n, (N_ℓ+N_u)/n); for each, fit on n and on
n−1 points with the *same seed*, and count how often the two predictions differ
by more than ε; compare against Binomial(⌊κ⌋, δ). Its power is exactly
α·((1−δ*_ε)/(1−δ))^{⌊min(B_train/n, N_ℓ/n, (N_ℓ+N_u)/n)⌋}, which nearly attains
Theorem 1 whenever B_train is small relative to |X| and |Y|. The same pattern
as `luo2026.md` Theorem 3.6: sophistication does not beat sample splitting when
the problem itself is hard.

**Result 5 — an unlimited budget does not save it, provided the test must
terminate.** Letting B_train → ∞ with finite stopping time, the computational
term vanishes but the statistical ones remain whenever |X| or |Y| is infinite
(p. 8). Real-valued features suffice.

**Result 6 (Theorem 3, Appendix A) — black-box models add a fourth
constraint.** When the fitted models can only be probed at finitely many
points, a fourth term appears involving B_eval, and with |X| = |Y| = ∞ the
bound collapses to α·((1−δ*_ε)/(1−δ))^{min(N_ℓ/n, (N_ℓ+N_u)/(n+1))} even for
arbitrarily large budgets.

**Result 7 — this strictly generalizes Kim & Barber (2023).** Their theorem
covers |Y| = ∞ with no computational accounting; it therefore does not apply to
ordinary binary classification with X = ℝᵈ and Y = {0, 1}. This paper's
finite-|Y| and budget terms are what close that hole (p. 8).

## Extracted values

The paper reports no simulations, no tables, and no worked numeric example. The
only constants that appear are the 0.2 mass threshold in Theorem 2 and the
unnamed universal constant C in the same bound.

## Bearing on nestedtune

- **It bears on exactly one thing: whether G6's stability blocker could ever be
  discharged empirically.** `tidymodels-nested-cv-gaps.md` records that
  `bayle2020.md`'s interval is valid only under loss stability, and that loss
  stability is unestablished for a tuned procedure. The natural question is
  whether a user, or the package, could *check* stability for their own
  workflow. This paper's answer for its notion of stability is no: any
  universally valid black-box certification needs either B_train ≫ n calls to
  the whole tune-and-fit procedure, or a data space small enough to search
  exhaustively. For a tidymodels workflow on real-valued predictors, neither
  holds.
- **But the match is not exact, and overclaiming here would be wrong.** The
  paper tests *prediction* perturbation ((ε, δ)-stability); `bayle2020.md`'s
  requirement is *loss* stability, which `luo2026.md` footnote 3 separately
  notes is strictly weaker than ℓ₂-stability. This paper's Discussion (p. 9)
  explicitly leaves a unified treatment of prediction-based and loss-based
  stability notions as an open question. So the honest statement for the G6 row
  is: *the closest studied analogue of the assumption `bayle2020.md` needs is
  provably uncertifiable from data alone; whether the hardness transfers to
  loss stability is open.* Not "stability is untestable, therefore G6 is
  closed".
- **It reinforces GP5 without adding a new blocker.** The G6 disposition
  already declines to ship an interval whose validity condition cannot be
  checked. This paper is the reason that condition is unlikely to become
  checkable, which turns a practical objection into a structural one.
- **Nothing here touches the package's own surface.** No estimator, no metric,
  no resampling scheme, no oracle. It constrains what a future feature could
  claim, nothing more.
- **One design observation worth keeping.** Result 4's test fits on n and on
  n−1 points *with the same random seed* to isolate the effect of the dropped
  observation. That is the same discipline D-011 imposes on nestedtune's fold
  seeds — draw the seed outside, hold it fixed, vary only the data — and it is
  what makes IP2's reproducibility contract a prerequisite for any future
  stability diagnostic rather than an independent nicety.

## Oracle status

**No oracle, and none possible.** As with `luo2026.md`, the results are upper
bounds on the power of hypothetical tests. There is no statistic to reproduce
and no published value to check an implementation against.

## Open questions

- Whether the hardness transfers from (ε, δ)-stability to the loss-based
  stability `bayle2020.md` requires. Named as open by the paper itself
  (Discussion, p. 9) — observed 2026-07-31.
- Whether a *partially* black-box setting helps, where part of the pipeline is
  known analytically. The paper raises this as a future direction (p. 9) and it
  is close to nestedtune's actual situation: the outer loop and the selection
  rule are fully transparent, and only the inner learner is opaque — observed
  2026-07-31.
- Whether the weaker data-conditional stability notion the paper points to
  (Kim & Barber 2023, §5.2) is testable, and whether any interval method needs
  only that. Neither source pursued here; Kim & Barber (2023) is not on the
  shelf — observed 2026-07-31.
- Whether this preprint has since appeared in a journal, which would change the
  citation — not checked as of 2026-07-31.
